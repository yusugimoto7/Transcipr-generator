Plates I–V · chronological
Plate I

The wrong train

March 2016 · Yokohama

You took the express by mistake and I followed you on so I wouldn't have to stop talking. We rode nine stops past where either of us was going, and neither of us mentioned it.

Plate II

The apartment with the bad radiator

November 2017 · Nakameguro

Two rooms, one window, and a heater that only worked if you hit it. We wore coats indoors all winter and called it camping.

Plate III

Yes, in the rain

June 2019 · Kamakura

I had a speech. The rain took it. You said yes before I found the ring, which is the part I tell people, because it's the part that matters.

Plate IV

Forty-one people, one long table

October 2020 · Hakone

Half the guest list couldn't come. The half that did stayed until two in the morning. Your father made a toast in English he had practiced for a month.

Plate V

The house with the lemon tree

April 2024 · Setagaya

It came with a tree that produces exactly four lemons a year. We keep them on the counter until they go bad, because throwing them out feels rude.

People ask how we met and I always give them the train, because it's short. The longer answer is that I met you four times before I noticed: once at a friend's birthday where you argued with me about a film neither of us had finished, twice in the lobby of a building where we both worked, and once in a photograph I didn't know you were in.

Ten years in, the story isn't the proposal. It's the fourteen thousand small decisions to keep choosing the same person.

What I did not know at twenty-six is that a marriage is mostly logistics, and that logistics can be a love language. You learned how I take my coffee before I told you. I learned which of your silences need company and which need a closed door.

This page exists because a decade deserves more than a folder on a hard drive. Everything here is yours. I just put it in order.
