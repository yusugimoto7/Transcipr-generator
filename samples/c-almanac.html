An almanac of ten years

Ren & Aoi,
season by season

Not a highlight reel — a record of what grew, and when. Kept the way you'd keep a garden journal: by the weather, by the year, by what was in bloom.

❦

Spring

2016
March

The wrong train

You took the express by mistake and I followed you on so I wouldn't have to stop talking. Nine stops past where either of us was going, and neither of us mentioned it.

May

The first thing we grew

Basil, on a windowsill, in a cup. It died in three weeks. We bought another one the same afternoon.

Winter

2017
November

Coats indoors

Two rooms, one window, and a radiator that only worked if you hit it. We wore coats indoors all winter and called it camping.

Summer

2019
June

Yes, in the rain

I had a speech. The rain took it. You said yes before I found the ring, which is the part I tell people, because it's the part that matters.

Autumn

2020
October

Forty-one people, one long table

Half the guest list couldn't come. The half that did stayed until two in the morning. Your father made a toast in English he had practiced for a month.

Spring

2024
April

Four lemons a year

The house came with a tree that produces exactly four lemons a year. We keep them on the counter until they go bad, because throwing them out feels rude.

❦

Aoi,

People ask how we met and I always give them the train, because it's short. The longer answer is that I met you four times before I noticed: at a friend's birthday, where you argued with me about a film neither of us had finished; twice in the lobby of a building where we both worked; and once in a photograph I didn't know you were in.

What I did not know at twenty-six is that a marriage is mostly logistics, and that logistics can be a love language. You learned how I take my coffee before I told you. I learned which of your silences need company and which need a closed door.

Ten years, and the thing I'd put in the almanac isn't a single day. It's that nothing here needed to be remarkable to be worth writing down.

— Ren
